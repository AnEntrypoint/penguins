# Galápagos Penguin

## Key Facts

- **Small penguin** - adults reach 1.6-1.9 feet tall and weigh 4-6 pounds
- **Habitat**: Galápagos Islands, Ecuador, primarily on Fernandina and the western coast of Isabela
- **Only equatorial penguin**: The only species whose range extends north of the equator in the wild
- **Scientific name**: *Spheniscus mendiculus*
- **Survives the tropics thanks to**: Cold, nutrient-rich water from the Humboldt and Cromwell currents
- **Distinctive markings**: Thin white line curving from the eye down to the throat; narrow black chest band
- **Diet**: Small schooling fish — mullet, sardines, anchovies — taken in cooperative inshore foraging
- **Breeding**: Opportunistic, breeding whenever sea-surface temperatures are cool and prey is abundant; nests in lava crevices and caves
- **El Niño vulnerability**: Warm-water El Niño events crash food supply and have repeatedly halved the population within a single season
- **Population**: Fewer than 2,000 individuals — the rarest penguin species
- **Lifespan**: 15-20 years
- **Conservation status**: Endangered; threats include El Niño frequency, introduced cats and rats, bycatch, and avian malaria
